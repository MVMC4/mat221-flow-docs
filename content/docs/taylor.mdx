A Taylor polynomial matches the value, slope, curvature, and higher derivatives of a function at a centre \(a\).

## The rule

$$
T_n(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x-a)^k.
$$

Maclaurin is the special case \(a=0\). The factorial is essential because differentiating \(x^k\) produces \(k!\).

## Worked approximation

For \(e^{0.2}\), use \(1+x+x^2/2+x^3/6\). The approximation is \(1.221333\). Bound the next term on the whole interval before reporting accuracy.

<div className="soft-callout"><strong>Common error.</strong> At a nonzero centre, every power is \((x-a)^k\), not \(x^k\).</div>

## Building series

Substitute into the known series for \(e^x\), \(\sin x\), \(\cos x\), \(1/(1-x)\), or \(\ln(1+x)\). Term-by-term differentiation and integration manufacture new series inside the radius of convergence.
