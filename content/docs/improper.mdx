An improper integral is a proper integral plus a limit. Cut off the infinite endpoint or singularity, evaluate, then ask whether the limit is finite.

## The p-test

$$
\int_1^\infty \frac{dx}{x^p}\text{ converges iff }p>1,\qquad
\int_0^1 \frac{dx}{x^p}\text{ converges iff }p<1.
$$

For $\int_0^2 1/(x-1)\,dx$, the singularity at $x=1$ forces a split. One divergent half is enough to make the full integral divergent; blind FTC evaluation is invalid.

## Comparison and transfer

For nonnegative functions, compare in the direction that preserves a known convergent or divergent benchmark. A doubly infinite integral must converge on both halves.

<div className="soft-callout"><strong>Worked example.</strong> $\int_1^\infty 1/x^2\,dx$ becomes $\lim_{t\to\infty}(1-1/t)=1$. Infinite width does not automatically mean infinite area.</div>

<div className="soft-callout"><strong>Common error.</strong> A symmetric cancellation across a singularity is not convergence. Split at every blow-up first.</div>
