A sequence is a list; a series is a sum of partial sums. Terms tending to zero is necessary for series convergence, not sufficient.

## Test selection

Use the divergence test first. Then recognize geometric and p-series forms, use ratio or root tests for factorials and powers, comparison for rational-like terms, and the alternating series test when signs alternate.

For $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1/n-1/(n+1))$, the partial sum is $1-1/(N+1)$, so the sum converges to $1$. The cancellation appears only after writing partial sums.

## Absolute versus conditional

If $\sum|a_n|$ converges, then $\sum a_n$ converges absolutely. The alternating harmonic series converges conditionally but not absolutely.
