Every application follows three moves: choose a slice, write the slice's contribution, and integrate over the correct bounds.

## Disks, washers, and shells

Perpendicular slices give disks or washers. Parallel slices give shells:

$$
V=\pi\int R^2\,dx,\qquad V=\pi\int(R^2-r^2)\,dx,\qquad V=2\pi\int(\text{radius})(\text{height})\,dx.
$$

For the region between $y=x^2$ and $y=2x$, the bounds are $0$ to $2$. About the $x$-axis, use washers with $R=2x$ and $r=x^2$. About the $y$-axis, use shells with radius $x$ and height $2x-x^2$.

## Length, surface area, and centroid

Arc length uses Pythagoras: $L=\int\sqrt{1+(y')^2}\,dx$. Surface area multiplies arc length by the swept circumference. Centroids use area-weighted coordinates.

<div className="soft-callout"><strong>Common error.</strong> Radius is distance to the axis; shell height is top minus bottom. Swapping them changes the geometry.</div>
