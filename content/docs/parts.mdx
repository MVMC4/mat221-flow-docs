Integration by parts is the product rule in reverse:

$$
\int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du.
$$

Choose $u$ so differentiation simplifies it, and $dv$ so integration is easy. LIATE is a useful heuristic: logarithmic, inverse trig, algebraic, trig, exponential.

## Worked example: a polynomial and an exponential

For $\int xe^x\,dx$, choose $u=x$ and $dv=e^x\,dx$. Then $du=dx$ and $v=e^x$:

$$
xe^x-\int e^x\,dx=(x-1)e^x+C.
$$

Differentiate the answer to check both the product and the sign.

## Tabular and cyclic cases

Differentiate the polynomial down to zero, integrate the other factor down, and alternate signs on the diagonals. For $e^x\sin x$, a second application reproduces the original integral. Name it $I$, collect the $I$-terms, and solve algebraically.
